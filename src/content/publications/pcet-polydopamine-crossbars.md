## Abstract & Research Highlights

Polydopamine (PDA) is an emerging biomimetic polymer exhibiting coupled electronic and protonic conduction. In this work, we demonstrate thin-film biopolymer crossbar memory devices ($\text{Au/PDA/Pt}$) operating via electric field-driven interfacial **proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET)**.

![PCET Mechanism and Experimental Summary](/academic-portfolio/images/projects/pda/pda_hero_summary.png)

### Key Contributions

- **Interfacial Switching Dynamics:** Unraveled how electric field-driven proton redistribution modulates volatile versus non-volatile resistance states across nanoscale electrodeposited thin films.
- **Variable-Temperature Transport:** Conducted low-temperature to room-temperature ($77\text{ K}\text{--}300\text{ K}$) electrical characterization to probe activation energies and electronic barrier height modulation.
- **Biological Synaptic Emulation:** Measured short-term plasticity (STP), paired-pulse facilitation (PPF), and relaxation dynamics under continuous pulse trains with low cycle-to-cycle variability.
- **Spectroscopic Verification:** Correlated catechol/quinone redox state transitions and protonation dynamics through XPS ($\text{N } 1s$, $\text{C } 1s$) and Raman spectroscopy.
