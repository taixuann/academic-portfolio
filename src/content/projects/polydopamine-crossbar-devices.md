## Project Overview

Polydopamine (PDA) is a biomimetic polymer exhibiting coupled electronic and protonic conduction. In this project, we design, fabricate, and characterize thin-film crossbar devices to elucidate the interfacial **proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET)** mechanism underlying analog resistance tuning and biological synaptic emulation.

![PDA Hero Summary](/images/projects/pda/pda_hero_summary.png)

## Core Research Questions

- How does electric field-driven proton dynamics govern volatile versus non-volatile resistive switching thresholds in nanoscale polydopamine thin films?
- Can protonation states be tuned to emulate biological synaptic plasticity metrics with low power and high cycling endurance?

## Key Contributions & Experimental Methods

- **Thin-Film Fabrication:** Engineered crossbar memory junctions ($\text{Au/PDA/Pt}$) by combining controlled potentiostatic electropolymerization of polydopamine with electron-beam evaporated metal top electrodes.
- **Electrical Transport:** Performed DC current-voltage ($I\text{--}V$) hysteresis sweeps, temperature-dependent electrical transport ($77\text{ K}\text{--}300\text{ K}$), and compliance-dependent switching characterization.
- **Synaptic Emulation:** Measured short-term plasticity (STP), paired-pulse facilitation (PPF), and relaxation dynamics under continuous pulse trains.
- **Spectroscopic Correlation:** Conducted Raman, UV–Vis, AFM morphology, and X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) to correlate catechol/quinone redox state transitions with memristive conduction states.
- **Measurement Automation:** Developed automated LabVIEW instrument control routines for continuous pulse-train acquisition, switching endurance, and retention evaluation.

## Outcomes & Impact

Demonstrated tunable analog switching with low cycle-to-cycle variability and short-term synaptic plasticity, forming the basis for an undergraduate thesis and a submitted manuscript to the *Journal of the American Chemical Society* (JACS, 2026).
