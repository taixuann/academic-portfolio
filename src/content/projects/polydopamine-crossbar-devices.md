## Project Overview

Polydopamine (PDA) is a biomimetic polymer exhibiting coupled electronic and protonic conduction. In this project, we design, fabricate, and characterize thin-film crossbar devices to elucidate the interfacial **proton-coupled electron transfer (PCET)** mechanism underlying analog resistance tuning and biological synaptic emulation.

![Experimental Summary Overview](/academic-portfolio/images/projects/pda/pda_hero_summary.png)

## Core Research Questions

- How does electric field-driven proton dynamics govern volatile versus non-volatile resistive switching thresholds and retention stability in nanoscale polydopamine thin films?
- Can protonation states and quinone/catechol redox transitions be leveraged to emulate biological synaptic plasticity metrics with low power and high cycling endurance?

## 1. Fabrication & Device Architecture

- **Active Layer Deposition:** Engineered thin-film crossbar junctions ($\text{Au/PDA/Pt}$) via controlled potentiostatic electropolymerization of polydopamine on lithographically patterned bottom electrodes.
- **Electrode Metallization:** Electron-beam evaporation of inert metal top contacts ($\text{Pt}$, $\text{Au}$) defining crossbar junctions with sub-micron thickness uniformity.

## 2. DC Switching & Resistive States

Current-voltage ($I\text{--}V$) sweeps demonstrate repeatable bipolar resistive switching, compliance-current dependent switching windows, and low cycle-to-cycle variability.

## 3. Variable-Temperature Transport & Conduction Physics

To probe electronic transport mechanisms and barrier heights, electrical measurements were conducted across low to room temperatures ($77\text{ K}\text{--}300\text{ K}$).

![Temperature-Dependent IV Overlay](/academic-portfolio/images/projects/pda/pda_temp_iv_overlay.png)

## 4. Pulse Plasticity & Synaptic Emulation

Transient fast-pulse measurements were deployed to evaluate bio-realistic synaptic plasticity, quantifying short-term plasticity (STP), paired-pulse facilitation (PPF), and frequency-dependent potentiation.

![PPF and STP Decay Curve](/academic-portfolio/images/projects/pda/pda_ppf_stp_decay.png)
*Measured Paired-Pulse Facilitation (PPF) index decay as a function of inter-pulse interval ($\Delta t$), emulating biological short-term synaptic plasticity.*

---

## 5. Structural & Chemical Spectroscopic Deconvolution

Comprehensive materials characterization was conducted to correlate catechol/quinone redox state transitions and protonation dynamics with measured conductance states:

- **X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS):** Core-level deconvolution of $\text{N } 1s$ and $\text{C } 1s$ spectra verifying amine, imine, and protonated species.
- **Raman Spectroscopy:** Tracking quinone/catechol vibrational modes before and after electrical cycling.
- **AFM & UV–Vis:** Surface morphology verification and optical bandgap estimation.

![XPS N 1s Core Level Spectrum](/academic-portfolio/images/projects/pda/pda_xps_n1s.png)
*High-resolution XPS $\text{N } 1s$ core-level deconvolution showing distinct chemical states corresponding to secondary amine ($-\text{NH}-$) and protonated amine species.*

![Raman Spectroscopy Comparison](/academic-portfolio/images/projects/pda/pda_raman.png)
*Raman spectrum confirming the characteristic polydopamine vibrational signatures ($1350\text{ cm}^{-1}$ and $1580\text{ cm}^{-1}$) and polymeric crosslinking.*
