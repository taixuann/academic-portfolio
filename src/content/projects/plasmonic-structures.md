## Project Overview

Localized surface plasmon resonance (LSPR) enables deep sub-wavelength optical field confinement and photothermal energy conversion. This project investigates electrodynamic near-field enhancement and coupled nanoscale heat dissipation in noble metal nanosphere dimer configurations using 3D finite-difference time-domain (FDTD) and finite-element thermal modeling.

## Core Research Questions

- How do core-shell shell thickness ratios modulate near-field hotspot enhancement in $\text{Au@Ag}$ dimers?
- What are the transient and steady-state thermal dissipation profiles under continuous-wave vs. pulsed optical excitation?

## Key Computational Contributions

- **3D Full-Wave Electrodynamic Simulations:** Built 3D FDTD models in Lumerical FDTD for homogeneous Au and bimetallic core-shell $\text{Au@Ag}$ nanosphere dimers with sub-nanometer mesh refinement in the interparticle gap.
- **Cross-Section Analysis:** Calculated spectral extinction, scattering, and absorption cross-sections across varying shell thicknesses ($1\text{--}10\text{ nm}$) and dielectric environments ($n = 1.0\text{--}1.5$).
- **Coupled Photothermal Dissipation:** Exported spatial absorption heat source profiles directly into Lumerical HEAT to simulate nanoscale temperature rise and spatial thermal dissipation in surrounding media.

## Outcomes & Numerical Findings

Established a validated simulation pipeline demonstrating that thin silver shells significantly enhance localized near-field intensity ($|E/E_0|^2$) while gold cores maintain stable photothermal dissipation pathways.
