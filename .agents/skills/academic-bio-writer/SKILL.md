# Academic Bio Writer

A disciplined skill for writing executive-level academic biography and homepage profiles for researchers, graduate students, postdocs, and PIs.

---

## 1. The Core Philosophy: The Profile is Not an SOP

An academic homepage is an **executive summary for peers, PIs, reviewers, and collaborators** who spend 15–30 seconds scanning your site.

| Dimension | Statement of Purpose (SOP) | Academic Homepage Profile |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Audience** | Admissions / Hiring committee | Global scientific community, peers, PIs, recruiters |
| **Primary Goal** | Demonstrate personal growth, grit, and motivation | Establish current identity, research questions, and capabilities |
| **Tone** | First-person reflective narrative, personal journey | Objective, confident, scholarly, precise |
| **Format** | Multi-page continuous essay | Modular: Executive Lead → Research Overview → Pillars → Milestones |
| **Common Mistake** | N/A | Copying personal struggles or narrative essays onto a public website |

---

## 2. Four-Pillar Profile Architecture

Every academic landing bio must follow this four-section hierarchy:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. Executive Lead (Standing + Core Domain)             │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. Scholarly Research Overview (Questions + Evidence)  │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. Research Pillars & Technical Matrix                 │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. Reverse-Chronological News & Milestones             │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Pillar 1: Executive Lead (1–2 Sentences)
- **Identity & Status:** Current position, department, institution, and notable academic standing (e.g., class valedictorian, fellowship holder, graduate researcher).
- **Core Scientific Domain:** The exact physical, chemical, computational, or engineering domain under investigation.

### Pillar 2: Scholarly Research Overview (2–3 Paragraphs)
- **Active Appointments / Labs:** Supervised research groups and visiting scholar appointments.
- **The Core Problem:** The fundamental physical or technical question you investigate (e.g., interfacial charge transport, kinetic relaxation, state switching).
- **Evidence of Experimental & Computational Rigor:** Concrete details that ground your competence without narrative fluff (e.g., number of devices fabricated, pulse cycles verified, temperatures achieved, modeling frameworks utilized).
- **Current Trajectory:** Forward-looking statement (e.g., preparing for doctoral studies, developing scalable architectures).

### Pillar 3: Research Pillars & Technical Matrix (3–4 Bullets)
- Avoid laundry lists of generic buzzwords.
- Group interests into distinct scientific or engineering pillars with high-density technical vocabulary.
- *Format:* `**[Domain / Mechanism]:** [Specific physical phenomena, materials systems, and methods]`.

### Pillar 4: News & Milestones (Reverse-Chronological)
- Clean timeline showing active academic momentum.
- Include: major awards, degree conferrals, visiting appointments, preprints/publications, and grant/application milestones.
- Keep each milestone to one clean, date-stamped line.

## 3. Strict Style & Anti-Pattern Rules

### Banned AI Clichés & Buzzwords
Never use these generic filler phrases:
- ❌ *"My research lies at the intersection of [A] and [B]..."* $\rightarrow$ Replace with direct statement: *"My research focuses on [A], [B], and [C]."*
- ❌ *"Bridging the gap between X and Y..."* $\rightarrow$ Replace with: *"Connecting fundamental [X] with scalable [Y]."*
- ❌ *"Delving into / exploring the vast possibilities of..."* $\rightarrow$ State the specific mechanism or hypothesis tested.
- ❌ *"Passionate researcher dedicated to..."* $\rightarrow$ State the track record and focus area directly.
- ❌ Narrative struggle prose: *"When I started, I spent weeks learning to..."* $\rightarrow$ Belongs in an essay, not on a professional site.

### Voice & Register
- **Register:** Scholarly, concise, authoritative, active voice.
- **Perspective:** First-person direct (`"I investigate..."`, `"My research focuses on..."`) or clean third-person if explicitly requested for lab directories.
- **Density:** High information density. Prefer physical specifics (e.g., *"electrochemical protonation in polydopamine crossbars"*) over abstractions (e.g., *"novel green nanotechnology"*).

## 4. Input Extraction & Synthesis Workflow

When distilling raw input (SOP, CV, paper abstracts, notes) into an academic bio:

```
Raw Inputs (CV / SOP / Notes)
          │
          ▼
1. Extract Hard Facts
   - Institution, rank, honors, degree date
   - Lab names, advisors, visiting stints
   - Physical materials systems, tools, measurements
          │
          ▼
2. Filter Out Narrative Essay Elements
   - Remove childhood stories, personal hardships, emotional musings
   - Retain technical breakthroughs, scale metrics, and verified results
          │
          ▼
3. Draft 4-Pillar Layout
   - Lead → Research Overview → Domain Pillars → News
          │
          ▼
4. Run Anti-Cliché Sweep
   - Eliminate filler verbs, hedge words, and generic transitions
```

## 6. Verification Checklist

Before publishing any academic bio:
- [ ] Is the lead sentence clear within 5 seconds of scanning?
- [ ] Are all academic claims, awards, and lab affiliations verified against source records?
- [ ] Are all narrative/personal struggle tropes removed?
- [ ] Are research interests structured with concrete physical terminology rather than general keywords?
- [ ] Is there an active, dated News section demonstrating current trajectory?
- [ ] Does the page compile cleanly in the static site generator (Astro/Hugo/Jekyll)?
